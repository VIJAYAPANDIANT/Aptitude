# Verbal Ability: Reading Comprehension

## 1. Core Question Types & Strategies

Reading Comprehension (RC) tests your ability to read, analyze, and interpret written information. The three most common question categories are:

### 1. Main Idea / Primary Purpose Questions
- **Goal**: Identify the central message, theme, or author's motive for writing the passage.
- **Strategy**: Look at the first and last paragraphs. Focus on the "thesis statement" (usually found near the beginning) rather than narrow supporting details in the middle paragraphs.
- **Wrong Answer Traps**: Options that are too broad (generalizing beyond the passage) or too narrow (focusing on a single detail/paragraph).

### 2. Inference Questions
- **Goal**: Draw a conclusion that is not explicitly stated in the text but is logically supported by the facts in the passage.
- **Strategy**: Locate the specific reference in the text, note the facts, and choose the option that *must* be true based on those facts.
- **Wrong Answer Traps**: Options that require leaps of speculation, statements that are directly copied from the passage (which is a fact, not an inference), or statements that introduce outside knowledge.

### 3. Tone of the Passage
- **Goal**: Identify the author's attitude towards the subject.
- **Common Tones**:
  - **Objective/Neutral/Analytical**: Unbiased, factual, logical reasoning without personal emotions.
  - **Sarcastic/Ironical/Cynical**: Mocking, disbelieving, or expressing skepticism.
  - **Laudatory/Eulogistic**: Praising and highly supportive.
  - **Critical/Skeptical**: Disapproving, raising doubts, or identifying flaws.
  - **Didactic**: Informative with an intent to teach or moralize.

**Read the following passage and answer the questions that follow:**

> While the technological advancements of the 21st century have undeniably streamlined communication and increased access to global markets, they have simultaneously birthed a insidious epidemic of digital exhaustion. Constant connectivity, once hailed as a liberation from the confines of the traditional nine-to-five office, has morphed into a psychological leash. Employees are now subtly coerced into responding to emails late at night, fearing that a delay will be misconstrued as a lack of professional commitment. This blur between professional availability and personal time has slowly eroded the cognitive downtime necessary for creative thought and mental restoration. Despite corporate wellness programs that offer yoga classes and meditation apps, the core issue remains unaddressed: the expectation of perpetual responsiveness. Until organizations dismantle the structural culture that rewards constant availability, burnout will continue to masquerade as individual failure rather than a systemic failure.

## 3. Step-by-Step Solutions

### Solution 1 (Main Idea)
- **Analyze the options**:
  - **A is too narrow**: While the author mentions wellness programs are insufficient, this is a supporting detail, not the main thesis.
  - **B is correct**: The passage focuses on how technology-driven constant connectivity creates workplace expectations that erode personal time and cause burnout, which is a systemic failure. This captures the entire text.
  - **C is a recommendation**: The author doesn't explicitly advise employees to take this action; the author argues that the structure of organizations must change.
  - **D is too extreme**: The author acknowledges the benefits of technology at the start ("undeniably streamlined communication"), so saying it does "more harm than good" is inaccurate.
- **Answer**: **B**

### Solution 2 (Inference)
- **Locate reference in the text**:
  - "This blur between professional availability and personal time has slowly eroded the cognitive downtime necessary for creative thought and mental restoration."
- **Analyze the options**:
  - If cognitive downtime is "necessary for creative thought and mental restoration", it means it is essential for these two outcomes.
  - **C** directly maps to this statement.
  - **A, B, and D** are either unsupported by the text or contradict the author's view.
- **Answer**: **C**

### Solution 3 (Tone)
- **Evaluate the language used by the author**:
  - The author uses strong words like "insidious epidemic", "psychological leash", "eroded", "masquerade", and "systemic failure".
  - This indicates a clear disapproval of the current corporate expectations and warns about its effects on mental health.
  - Therefore, the tone is **Critical and warning**.
- **Answer**: **B**

### Solution 2 (Passage 2)
