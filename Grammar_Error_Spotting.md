# Verbal Ability: Grammar & Error Spotting

## 1. Core Grammar Rules & Common Errors

### 1. Subject-Verb Agreement (SVA)
- **Basic Rule**: A singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb.
  - *Error*: "The list of items are on the desk." $\to$ *Correct*: "The list of items **is** on the desk." (The subject is the singular "list", not the plural "items").
- **Conjunctions (`Either/Or`, `Neither/Nor`)**: The verb agrees with the subject closest to it.
  - *Example*: "Neither the teacher nor the students **are** present."
  - *Example*: "Neither the students nor the teacher **is** present."
- **Indefinite Pronouns**: Words like *each, everyone, everybody, someone, nobody* are always singular.
  - *Example*: "Each of the players **has** (not have) performed well."

### 2. Tenses
- **Past Perfect Rule**: When two actions happened in the past, the earlier action takes the Past Perfect tense ($had + V_3$), and the later action takes the Simple Past tense ($V_2$).
  - *Error*: "The train left before I reached the station."
  - *Correct*: "The train **had left** before I reached the station."
- **Since/For with Perfect Tenses**: Use Present Perfect Continuous for actions starting in the past and continuing now.
  - *Error*: "I am living here since five years."
  - *Correct*: "I **have been living** here **for** five years."

### 3. Articles (A, An, The)
- **Vowel Sound vs. Vowel Letter**: Use "an" before a vowel *sound*, not just a vowel *letter*.
  - *Error*: "An university student." $\to$ *Correct*: "**A** university student." (The "u" sounds like a consonant "yoo").
  - *Error*: "A honest man." $\to$ *Correct*: "**An** honest man." (The "h" is silent, sounding like "on-est").

### 4. Prepositions
- Avoid common preposition redundancies or errors:
  - *Error*: "He discussed about the problem." $\to$ *Correct*: "He discussed the problem." ("discuss" does not take "about").
  - *Error*: "She is good in mathematics." $\to$ *Correct*: "She is good **at** mathematics."

## 2. Practice Problems

Find the part of the sentence that contains a grammatical error. If there is no error, choose "No Error".

### Problem 1
> (A) Neither the manager / (B) nor the employees was / (C) aware of the security breach. / (D) No Error

### Problem 2
> (A) If I would have worked harder, / (B) I would have / (C) passed the examination. / (D) No Error

### Problem 3
> (A) The patient died / (B) before the doctor / (C) arrived at the hospital. / (D) No Error

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Solutions

### Solution 1
1. **Analyze the structure**:
   - The subject uses the correlative conjunction "Neither... nor".
   - The subjects are "the manager" (singular) and "the employees" (plural).
2. **Apply the rule**:
   - For "Neither... nor", the verb must agree with the closer subject.
   - The closer subject is "the employees", which is plural.
   - Therefore, the verb should be plural ("were" instead of "was").
3. **Answer**: **B** (should be "were")

### Solution 2
1. **Analyze the conditional structure**:
   - This is a third conditional sentence (expressing a hypothetical past action and its result).
   - Structure: `If + Past Perfect` (condition clause), `would have + V3` (result clause).
2. **Apply the rule**:
   - The condition clause should use the Past Perfect tense ("had worked"), not "would have worked".
   - Correct sentence: "If I **had worked** harder, I would have passed the examination."
3. **Answer**: **A** (should be "If I had worked")

### Solution 3
1. **Analyze the sequence of past events**:
   - Two past events: The patient dying and the doctor arriving.
   - The patient dying happened first. The doctor arriving happened second.
2. **Apply the rule**:
   - The earlier event must be in the Past Perfect tense: "The patient **had died**".
   - The later event must be in the Simple Past tense: "arrived" (already correct).
   - Correct sentence: "The patient **had died** before the doctor arrived..."
3. **Answer**: **A** (should be "The patient had died")
