# Quantitative Aptitude: Profit, Loss & Discount

## 1. Concept Definitions & Explanations

- **Cost Price (CP)**: The price at which an article is purchased.
- **Selling Price (SP)**: The price at which an article is sold.
- **Profit (Gain)**: Occurs when Selling Price is greater than Cost Price ($SP > CP$).
- **Loss**: Occurs when Cost Price is greater than Selling Price ($CP > SP$).
- **Marked Price (MP / List Price)**: The price printed/labeled on the article.
- **Discount**: The reduction offered on the Marked Price.
- **Markup**: The increase in price above the Cost Price to set the Marked Price.

---

## 2. Key Formulas & Shortcuts

### Basic Calculations
- **Profit** $= SP - CP$
- **Loss** $= CP - SP$
- **Profit %** $= \left( \frac{\text{Profit}}{CP} \times 100 \right)\%$
- **Loss %** $= \left( \frac{\text{Loss}}{CP} \times 100 \right)\%$
- **Discount** $= MP - SP$
- **Discount %** $= \left( \frac{\text{Discount}}{MP} \times 100 \right)\%$

### SP and CP Relations
- **Finding SP**:
  - When there is a Profit: $SP = CP \times \left( \frac{100 + \text{Profit}\%}{100} \right)$
  - When there is a Loss: $SP = CP \times \left( \frac{100 - \text{Loss}\%}{100} \right)$
- **Finding CP**:
  - When there is a Profit: $CP = \frac{SP \times 100}{100 + \text{Profit}\%}$
  - When there is a Loss: $CP = \frac{SP \times 100}{100 - \text{Loss}\%}$

### MP to CP Ratio Shortcut (Crucial)
$$\frac{MP}{CP} = \frac{100 + \text{Profit}\%}{100 - \text{Discount}\%}$$

### Successive Discounts
If successive discounts of $d_1\%$ and $d_2\%$ are given, the equivalent single discount is:
$$\text{Net Discount} = \left( d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 \times d_2}{100} \right)\%$$

### Dishonest Dealer / Faulty Weights
When a merchant claims to sell at Cost Price but uses a faulty weight:
$$\text{Gain \%} = \frac{\text{Error}}{\text{True Value} - \text{Error}} \times 100$$
*Alternatively: $\text{Gain \%} = \left( \frac{\text{True Weight}}{\text{False Weight}} - 1 \right) \times 100$*

## 3. Practice Problems

### Problem 1
An article is sold at a profit of 20%. If it had been sold for Rs. 60 less, there would have been a loss of 10%. Find the Cost Price of the article.

### Problem 2
Find the single discount equivalent to a series of successive discounts of 20%, 10%, and 5%.

### Problem 3
A dishonest shopkeeper sells sugar at cost price but uses a false weight of 900 grams instead of 1 kilogram. What is his overall profit percentage?

---

## 4. Step-by-Step Solutions

### Solution 1
1. **Represent the situations using CP**:
   - Let Cost Price be $CP$.
   - Selling Price at 20% profit $= 1.20 \times CP$.
   - Selling Price at 10% loss $= 0.90 \times CP$.
2. **Set up the equation**:
   - The difference between the two selling prices is Rs. 60.
   $$1.20 \times CP - 0.90 \times CP = 60$$
   $$0.30 \times CP = 60$$
   $$CP = \frac{60}{0.30} = 200$$
3. **Answer**: The Cost Price is **Rs. 200**.

### Solution 2
1. **Combine the first two discounts (20% and 10%)**:
   $$\text{Equivalent Discount } (D_{12}) = 20 + 10 - \frac{20 \times 10}{100} = 30 - 2 = 28\%$$
2. **Combine the result with the third discount (28% and 5%)**:
   $$\text{Equivalent Discount } (D_{123}) = 28 + 5 - \frac{28 \times 5}{100} = 33 - 1.4 = 31.6\%$$
3. **Answer**: The equivalent single discount is **31.6%**.

### Solution 3
1. **Identify the values**:
   - True Value $= 1000$ grams.
   - Faulty Value $= 900$ grams.
   - Error $= 1000 - 900 = 100$ grams.
2. **Apply the Dishonest Dealer Formula**:
   $$\text{Gain \%} = \frac{\text{Error}}{\text{True Value} - \text{Error}} \times 100$$
   $$\text{Gain \%} = \frac{100}{900} \times 100 = \frac{100}{9}\% = 11.11\%$$
3. **Answer**: His overall profit is **11.11%** (or $11\frac{1}{9}\%$).
