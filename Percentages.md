# Quantitative Aptitude: Percentages

## 1. Concept Definitions & Explanations

- **Percentage**: A fraction with a denominator of 100, represented by the symbol `%`.
- **Percentage Change**: The relative change of a value expressed as a fraction of 100.
- **Base Value**: The original number before any increase or decrease. It is always the denominator in a percentage change formula.

---

## 2. Key Formulas & Shortcuts

### Conversions
- **Percentage to Fraction**: Divide by 100. E.g., $40\% = \frac{40}{100} = \frac{2}{5}$.
- **Fraction to Percentage**: Multiply by 100. E.g., $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{4} \times 100 = 75\%$.

### Percentage Increase/Decrease
$$\text{Percentage Increase} = \frac{\text{Increase}}{\text{Original Value}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Percentage Decrease} = \frac{\text{Decrease}}{\text{Original Value}} \times 100$$

### Product Stability Rule (Comparison)
- If $A$ is $x\%$ more than $B$, then $B$ is less than $A$ by:
  $$\left( \frac{x}{100 + x} \times 100 \right)\%$$
- If $A$ is $x\%$ less than $B$, then $B$ is more than $A$ by:
  $$\left( \frac{x}{100 - x} \times 100 \right)\%$$

### Successive Percentage Change
If a quantity is increased/decreased by $a\%$ and then by $b\%$, the net percentage change is:
$$\text{Net Change} = \left( a + b + \frac{a \times b}{100} \right)\%$$
*Note: Use positive values for increase and negative values for decrease.*

### Population / Depreciation Formula
Let the present value or population be $P$, and the rate of growth/depreciation be $R\%$ per annum.
- **Value/Population after $n$ years**:
  $$P_n = P \left( 1 \pm \frac{R}{100} \right)^n$$
- **Value/Population $n$ years ago**:
  $$P_0 = \frac{P}{\left( 1 \pm \frac{R}{100} \right)^n}$$
*Note: Use $+$ for growth/appreciation and $-$ for decay/depreciation.*

## 3. Practice Problems

### Problem 1
If the price of petrol increases by 25%, by what percentage must a motorist reduce his consumption so that his expenditure remains unchanged?

### Problem 2
A man spends 75% of his income. His income increases by 20% and his expenditure increases by 15%. By what percentage do his savings increase?

### Problem 3
The length of a rectangle is increased by 20% and its breadth is decreased by 10%. Find the net percentage change in the area of the rectangle.

---

## 4. Step-by-Step Solutions

### Solution 1
1. **Identify the percentage increase**:
   - The price of petrol increases by $x = 25\%$.
2. **Apply the Product Stability Rule**:
   - To keep expenditure constant, the reduction in consumption is:
     $$\text{Reduction} = \left( \frac{x}{100 + x} \times 100 \right)\% = \left( \frac{25}{125} \times 100 \right)\% = \left( \frac{1}{5} \times 100 \right)\% = 20\%$$
3. **Answer**: The consumption must be reduced by **20%**.

### Solution 2
1. **Assume initial values**:
   - Let initial Income $= 100$.
   - Expenditure $= 75\%$ of $100 = 75$.
   - Savings $= \text{Income} - \text{Expenditure} = 100 - 75 = 25$.
2. **Calculate new values after increases**:
   - New Income $= 100 + 20\% \text{ of } 100 = 120$.
   - New Expenditure $= 75 + 15\% \text{ of } 75 = 75 + 11.25 = 86.25$.
   - New Savings $= 120 - 86.25 = 33.75$.
3. **Calculate percentage increase in savings**:
   - Increase in savings $= 33.75 - 25 = 8.75$.
   - Percentage Increase $= \frac{8.75}{25} \times 100 = 8.75 \times 4 = 35\%$.
4. **Answer**: Savings increase by **35%**.

### Solution 3
1. **Identify changes**:
   - Length increase: $a = +20\%$
   - Breadth decrease: $b = -10\%$
2. **Apply the Successive Percentage Change Formula**:
   $$\text{Net Change} = \left( a + b + \frac{a \times b}{100} \right)\%$$
   $$\text{Net Change} = 20 - 10 + \frac{20 \times (-10)}{100} = 10 - 2 = 8\%$$
3. **Interpret result**:
   - The positive sign indicates an increase.
4. **Answer**: The area increases by **8%**.
