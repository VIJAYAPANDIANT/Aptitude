# Verbal Ability: Idioms, Phrases & One-Word Substitutions

## 1. High-Frequency Reference Lists

### 1. Common Idioms & Meanings
- **Bite the bullet**: To face a difficult situation with courage and get it over with.
- **Spill the beans**: To reveal a secret prematurely or accidentally.
- **Burn the midnight oil**: To study or work late into the night.
- **Take with a grain of salt**: To view something with skepticism or not take it literally.
- **Under the weather**: Feeling slightly sick or unwell.
- **Blessing in disguise**: A misfortune that eventually results in a good outcome.

### 2. Common Phrasal Verbs
- **Call off**: To cancel something (e.g., "They called off the meeting").
- **Look into**: To investigate (e.g., "The police are looking into the matter").
- **Give in**: To surrender or yield to pressure (e.g., "He finally gave in to their demands").
- **Bring about**: To cause something to happen (e.g., "New policies brought about economic changes").

### 3. Key One-Word Substitutions
- **Altruist**: A person unselfishly concerned for or devoted to the welfare of others.
- **Misanthrope**: A person who dislikes and avoids human society.
- **Insolvent / Bankrupt**: A person who is unable to pay their debts.
- **Omniscient**: One who knows everything.
- **Ornithology**: The scientific study of birds.
- **Ephemeral**: Lasting for a very short time.

## 2. Practice Problems

### Problem 1 (Idioms)
Choose the option that best expresses the meaning of the highlighted idiom:
> The startup was struggling financially, but landing the major government contract was a **blessing in disguise** as it forced them to streamline their operations.
A) A complete disaster that could not be repaired.
B) An apparent misfortune that eventually turned out to be highly beneficial.
C) A stroke of luck that required no effort.
D) An event that caused confusion and panic.

### Problem 2 (One-Word Substitution)
Find the single word that can substitute the given phrase:
> **A person who thinks only of himself and his own self-interest.**
A) Altruist
B) Egotist
C) Philanthropist
D) Polyglot

### Problem 3 (Phrasal Verbs)
Fill in the blank with the correct phrasal verb:
> The management decided to __________ the launch of the new product until market conditions improved.
A) put off
B) call off
C) bring up
D) turn down

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Solutions

### Solution 1
1. **Define the idiom**:
   - A **blessing in disguise** is something that seems bad or unlucky at first, but results in something good happening later.
2. **Evaluate the options**:
   - Option B matches this definition ("apparent misfortune that eventually turned out to be highly beneficial").
3. **Answer**: **B**

### Solution 2
1. **Define the options**:
   - **Altruist**: One who helps others unselfishly (opposite of self-interest).
   - **Egotist**: A self-centered or self-interested person; someone who thinks only of themselves.
   - **Philanthropist**: A person who seeks to promote the welfare of others, especially by donating money.
   - **Polyglot**: A person who knows or uses several languages.
2. **Match with the definition**:
   - The phrase describes an **Egotist**.
3. **Answer**: **B**

### Solution 3
1. **Define the phrasal verbs**:
   - **Put off**: To postpone or delay (fits "until market conditions improved").
   - **Call off**: To cancel (implies it won't happen at all, whereas "until" indicates postponement).
   - **Bring up**: To mention or raise a child.
   - **Turn down**: To reject or decrease volume.
2. **Select the correct verb**:
   - The sentence refers to delaying the launch, so "**put off**" is the correct fit.
3. **Answer**: **A**
